7. Information Requests and File Inspection / Copy

At the request of the parent(s)/guardian, the healthcare provider provides information about the minor, or inspection of or a copy of data in the minor's file, insofar as this information or data is, in the healthcare provider's opinion, relevant for decisions by the parent(s)/guardian about the minor's treatment. In doing so, the healthcare provider takes into account the limitations in connection with good caremanship. The healthcare provider discusses the provision of information with the minor.

The healthcare provider provides information, or inspection of or a copy of data in the file, only with the minor's explicit consent if, in the healthcare provider's opinion, this information or data is not relevant for decisions about the minor's treatment.

If the healthcare provider provides information or a copy of data from the minor's file at the request of one parent, the healthcare provider also gives it to the other parent with authority.

If the parent(s)/guardian exercise(s) a right to a copy of or inspection of the file, the healthcare provider responds within one month of the parent(s)/guardian's request. If the healthcare provider needs more time to comply with the request, the healthcare provider informs the parent(s)/guardian within one month, stating the timeframe within which a response can be expected. The healthcare provider adheres to the maximum extension period of two months.

8. Provision of Information to Parent Without Authority

The healthcare provider provides general and factual information about the treatment of the minor at the request of a parent without authority, unless:

  • the healthcare provider would not provide the information to the parent with authority, or
  • the best interests of the minor oppose the provision of the information.

The healthcare provider discusses the request with the minor and, if necessary, with the parent with authority. Prior to this, the healthcare provider informs the parent without authority of this discussion.

9. Treatment File

9.1 File Content

a. The healthcare provider makes a report of every treatment session and keeps it in the file. The records show, among other things:

  • which examinations were performed and what their outcomes were;
  • which treatment was given and what its effects were.

b. In addition, the healthcare provider makes notes in the file of:

  • the information provided by the healthcare provider to the parent(s)/guardian and minor;
  • the consent of the parent(s)/guardian and minor for a treatment;
  • the information provided by the parent(s)/guardian and the minor;
  • the evaluation of the treatment;
  • any further agreements on the content or modification of the treatment;
  • consent of the minor and of the parent(s)/guardian for providing information about the minor or the parent(s)/guardian respectively to third parties;
  • telephone conversations with the parent(s)/guardian and minor, insofar as relevant to the treatment and/or treatment relationship or to accountability.

c. The healthcare provider retains correspondence in the file:

  • with the parent(s)/guardian and minor, insofar as relevant to the treatment and/or treatment relationship or to accountability for the treatment;
  • with others, healthcare providers or health insurers.

d. In addition to the information described under a to c, the healthcare provider retains all other information relevant to the treatment or to accountability.

9.2 File Retention

The healthcare provider retains the file for 20 years from the date of the last modification in the file, unless longer retention is required due to good caremanship.

10. Confidentiality and Data Handling

10.1 Confidentiality

The healthcare provider keeps confidential all information about the minor and the parent(s)/guardian obtained in that capacity. This information is provided to third parties (other than the minor) only if there is a ground recognised in law or case law for breaching the duty of confidentiality, the most important of which are:

  • explicit consent from the minor;
  • a legal obligation (if statutory conditions are met);
  • a statutory reporting right (if statutory conditions are met).

Exceptions to the duty of confidentiality are:

  1. provision of information to the parent(s)/guardian of the minor;
  2. providing (access) to substitutes or replacements of the healthcare provider, insofar as necessary for the care of the minor;
  3. providing (access) to persons directly involved in executing the treatment agreement, insofar as they need this information for their work/tasks.

10.2 Exchange of Information with GP & Other Healthcare Providers

Information about the minor is shared orally or in writing with the GP or other healthcare providers (outside the healthcare provider's practice) exclusively with the minor's consent, with the exception of information to substitutes and replacements of the healthcare provider. Providing information about the parent(s)/guardian requires their consent.

10.3 GDPR Compliance

The healthcare provider complies with obligations under the GDPR (AVG) to protect the data of the minor and the parent(s)/guardian. The privacy statement on the practice website describes how their data is handled and what rights the minor and the parent(s)/guardian have under the GDPR.

11. Accessibility and Availability

The healthcare provider is accessible only during business days at the times listed on the practice website.

Outside these times, the healthcare provider is not accessible. For questions or complaints that cannot wait until the healthcare provider is available again, the client should contact their general practitioner (or GP out-of-hours service) or another healthcare provider involved in their care.

14.1 Payment Term

The payment term for invoices is days. In the absence of payment within this term, the parent(s)/guardian is/are in default by operation of law. If the parent(s)/guardian do(es) not pay the invoice after repeated reminders, the healthcare provider may engage an external collection agency.

14.2 Cancellation Policy

If the minor cancels within hours prior to the appointment or fails to appear, the parent(s)/guardian owe(s) the agreed fee.

15. Termination of Agreement

15.1 Termination by Parent(s)/Guardian

The parent(s)/guardian can terminate the treatment agreement at any time.

15.2 Termination by the Healthcare Provider

The healthcare provider may unilaterally terminate the treatment agreement if there is a compelling reason, observing all applicable requirements of due care.

16. Complaints Procedure

If a parent or guardian is not satisfied with the healthcare provider or staff in the practice, they discuss this with the healthcare provider where possible. The healthcare provider gives the parent/guardian room to tell their story, explains on request and reflects on his/her own conduct.

If a conversation does not resolve the dissatisfaction, the healthcare provider informs the parent/guardian of the possibility, with authorisation from the minor, of filing a complaint under the healthcare provider's Wkkgz complaints procedure, which can be found at https://www.poojakarnik.com/klachtenregeling.html, or, if applicable, of submitting a complaint to a complaints committee under the Youth Act (Jeugdwet).

Following the complaints procedure under the Wkkgz, the parent/guardian may, with authorisation from the minor, ask the disputes body to which the healthcare provider is affiliated for a ruling. In that case, the parent/guardian and the healthcare provider undertake to follow the binding advice of the disputes body.
