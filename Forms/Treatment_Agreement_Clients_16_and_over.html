5. Treatment File

5.1 File Content

a. The healthcare provider makes a report of every treatment session and keeps it in the file. The records show, among other things:

  • which examinations were performed and what their outcomes were;
  • which treatment was given and what its effects were.

b. In addition, the healthcare provider makes notes in the file of:

  • the information provided by the healthcare provider;
  • consent given by the client for a treatment;
  • the information provided by the client;
  • the evaluation of the treatment;
  • any further agreements on the content or modification of the treatment;
  • the client's consent for providing information to third parties;
  • telephone conversations with the client, insofar as relevant to the treatment and/or treatment relationship or to accountability.

c. The healthcare provider retains correspondence in the file:

  • with the client, insofar as relevant to the treatment and/or treatment relationship or to accountability for the treatment;
  • with others, such as healthcare providers or health insurers.

d. In addition to the information described under a to c, the healthcare provider retains all other information relevant to the treatment or to accountability.

5.2 File Retention

The healthcare provider retains the file for 20 years from the date of the last modification in the file.

6. Confidentiality and Data Handling

6.1 Confidentiality

The healthcare provider keeps confidential all information about the client obtained in his/her capacity as healthcare provider. This information is provided to third parties (other than the client) only if there is a ground recognised in law or case law for breaching the duty of confidentiality, the most important of which are:

  • explicit consent from the client;
  • a legal obligation (if statutory conditions are met);
  • a statutory reporting right (if statutory conditions are met).

Exceptions to the duty of confidentiality are:

  • provision of information to the representative of a client who is not legally competent;
  • providing (access) to substitutes or replacements of the healthcare provider, insofar as necessary for the care of the client;
  • providing (access) to persons directly involved in executing the treatment agreement, insofar as they need this information for their work/tasks.

6.2 Exchange of Information with GP & Other Healthcare Providers

Information about the client is shared orally or in writing with the GP or other healthcare providers (outside the healthcare provider's practice) exclusively with the client's consent, with the exception of substitutes and replacements of the healthcare provider.

6.3 GDPR Compliance

The healthcare provider complies with obligations under the GDPR (AVG) to protect the client's data. The privacy statement on the practice website describes how data is handled and what rights the client has under the GDPR.

7. Exercise of the Client's Rights Regarding Data in the File

If the client exercises a right regarding data in his/her file, the healthcare provider responds within one month of the client's request. If the healthcare provider needs more time to comply with the request, he/she informs the client within one month, stating the timeframe within which the client can expect a response. The healthcare provider adheres to the maximum extension period of two months.

8. Accessibility and Availability

The healthcare provider is accessible only during business days at the times listed on the practice website.

Outside these times, the healthcare provider is not accessible. For questions or complaints that cannot wait until the healthcare provider is available again, the client should contact their general practitioner (or GP out-of-hours service) or another healthcare provider involved in their care.

12.1 Termination by the Client

13. Complaints Procedure

If the client is not satisfied with the conduct of the healthcare provider or staff in the practice, the client first tries to discuss this with the healthcare provider. The healthcare provider gives the client room to tell their story, answers the client's questions and reflects on his/her own conduct.

If a conversation does not resolve the dissatisfaction, the healthcare provider informs the client of the possibility of filing a complaint under the healthcare provider's Wkkgz complaints procedure, which can be found at https://www.poojakarnik.com/klachtenregeling.html, or, if applicable, of submitting a complaint to the complaints committee under the Youth Act (Jeugdwet).

Following the complaints procedure under the Wkkgz, the client may ask the disputes body to which the healthcare provider is affiliated for a ruling. In that case, the client and the healthcare provider undertake to follow the binding advice of the disputes body.
